Fremont is five towns

Fremont was incorporated in 1956 from five towns: Centerville, Irvington, Mission San Jose, Niles and Warm Springs. The names still mark the neighborhoods. Pick one on the map.

Five towns, one city

Fremont was incorporated on January 23, 1956, when the five towns unified into one city. Central Park, Lake Elizabeth and the Fremont BART station sit in the middle, between them.

Niles

At the mouth of Niles Canyon, where Alameda Creek comes out of the hills.

Name
From the Central Pacific Railroad's Niles junction, opened in 1870 and named for railroad attorney Addison Niles
Film history
Essanay Studios made films here from 1912 to 1916, including Charlie Chaplin's The Tramp (1915)
Today
The Niles Essanay Silent Film Museum screens silent films, and the Niles Canyon Railway runs excursion trains up the canyon

Sources: Fremont, California and Essanay Studios (Wikipedia); Movies in Niles (Museum of Local History)

Centerville

North-central Fremont, between I-880 and the BART line.

Name
The post office spelled it "Centreville" until 1893
Rail
Fremont–Centerville station serves ACE trains and Amtrak's Capitol Corridor
Landmarks
Quarry Lakes Regional Recreation Area, and Centerville Pioneer Cemetery, where many of the area's pioneers are buried

Source: Fremont, California (Wikipedia)

Irvington

South-central Fremont, from Mowry Avenue south to Auto Mall Parkway.

Name
Named Irving in 1884, after a circuit judge; the name later became Irvington
Landmark
Five Corners, the intersection of Fremont and Washington Boulevards, is marked at Irvington Plaza park

Source: Fremont, California (Wikipedia)

Mission San Jose

At the base of the eastern hills, below Mission Peak.

History
Mission San José, founded in 1797, was the fourteenth Spanish mission in California
Landmark
The mission church standing today is a reconstruction, dedicated in 1985
Hills
Mission Peak rises directly to the east

Sources: Mission San José (California) and Fremont, California (Wikipedia)

Warm Springs

The southern end of Fremont, where it meets Milpitas.

Name
From the warm springs on Rancho Agua Caliente; the Harrisburgh post office was renamed Warm Springs in 1885
BART
Warm Springs/South Fremont station opened on March 25, 2017
Roads
State Route 262 links I-880 and I-680 here

Sources: Fremont, California and Warm Springs/South Fremont station (Wikipedia)

How it works now

Buying and selling changed in 2024 and 2025: buyers now sign a written agreement before touring, and every agent's pay is spelled out up front. Here's the whole path. Check things off as you go; your list stays in this browser.

  1. Talk it through

    A first conversation about what you want, where, and when. It costs nothing.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

  2. Get pre-approved

    A lender reviews your income, assets and credit and tells you how much you can borrow. A pre-approval makes an offer stronger. You pick the lender.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

  3. Sign a buyer agreement

    Before I show you a home, you and I sign a written buyer agreement. It states exactly how I'm paid. In California it can't run longer than three months without a renewal signed by both of us, and agent pay isn't set by law: it's negotiable.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

  4. Tour and make an offer

    I set up tours, pull recent comparable sales, and write your offer with the price and terms that protect you.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

  5. Escrow and inspections

    Once your offer is accepted, escrow opens. You deposit earnest money, get inspections, and your lender orders an appraisal, all within the deadlines in your contract.

    Checklist 0 of 3 done

  6. Close

    You sign the loan and escrow documents, funds move through escrow, the deed records, and the keys are yours. Expect a supplemental property tax bill afterward. Never wire money on the strength of an email alone: call your escrow officer at a number you already have.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

    Escrow pays off your loan, collects the documentary transfer tax, and records the deed. You hand over the keys at closing. Confirm any wiring instructions by phone with your escrow officer before money moves.
