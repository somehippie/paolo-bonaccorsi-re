Primary focus on Fremont, including Antelope Hills, Mission San Jose and coverage across the broader East Bay.

Fremont is five towns

Fremont was incorporated in 1956 from five towns: Centerville, Irvington, Mission San Jose, Niles and Warm Springs. The names still mark the neighborhoods. Its neighbors went other ways: Newark incorporated on its own, Alvarado and Decoto joined as Union City, and Sunol never incorporated at all. Pick a place on the map.

Five towns, one city

Fremont was incorporated on January 23, 1956, when the five towns unified into one city. Central Park, Lake Elizabeth and the Fremont BART station sit in the middle, between them.

Incorporated
January 23, 1956
Name
For John C. Frémont, who led an 1846 expedition through Mission Pass and later served as a U.S. senator from California
Before 1956
The five towns were part of Washington Township, formed in 1853. Wally Pond chaired the incorporation committee

78.3 square miles, plus 10.1 of water
Population
230,504 (2020 Census)
BART

Neighbors
Newark (incorporated 1955), Union City (1959) and Sunol (unincorporated)

Niles

At the mouth of Niles Canyon, where Alameda Creek comes out of the hills.

Rancho era
José de Jesús Vallejo, granted Rancho Arroyo de la Alameda in 1842, built a flour mill at the canyon mouth. The settlement was first called Vallejo Mills
Railroad
In September 1869 the Central Pacific finished the transcontinental link from Sacramento to the bay through the canyon. The Niles junction opened in April 1870, named for railroad attorney Addison Niles, later a California Supreme Court justice
Nursery
The California Nursery Company was incorporated here in 1884 by John Rock and partners and became the largest firm of its kind west of the Rockies. The Vallejo adobe, its guest lodge, is on the National Register, and the grounds are now California Nursery Historic Park
Film history
Essanay Studios made films here from 1912 to 1916, including Charlie Chaplin’s The Tramp (1915) and Broncho Billy Anderson westerns
Today
Old Town Niles keeps its own library and post office. The Niles Essanay Silent Film Museum screens silent films, and the Niles Canyon Railway runs excursion trains up the canyon to Sunol

Sources: Fremont, California, Washington Township, California Nursery Company and Essanay Studios (Wikipedia); Movies in Niles (Museum of Local History)

Centerville

North-central Fremont, between I-880 and the BART line. It was the main town of Washington Township, the 1853 county division, named for George Washington, that covered today’s Fremont, Newark and Union City.

Beginnings
George Lloyd sold cold beer to stage passengers from a tent here in 1850. Captain George Bond opened a general store, and the name Centerville was chosen
Name
The post office opened as “Centreville” in 1855 and took the American spelling in 1893
Schools
Washington High School, Fremont’s oldest high school, was established in 1891. Its 1935 building is on the National Register
Farms
Orchards ran across the district; the Freitas Ranch on Thornton Avenue grew acres of apricots. Japanese American farming families here were forced out under Executive Order 9066 during World War II
Cannery fire
In 1959 a two-day fire destroyed the cannery on Baine Avenue, the largest fire in Fremont’s history. It was never rebuilt
Today
Centerville Pioneer Cemetery holds many of the area’s founding pioneers. Fremont–Centerville station serves ACE and Amtrak’s Capitol Corridor, and Quarry Lakes is nearby

Sources: Fremont, California, Washington Township and Washington High School (Wikipedia)

Irvington

South-central Fremont, from Mowry Avenue south to Auto Mall Parkway, around the old crossroads called Five Corners.

First building
In the early 1850s two emancipated Black men traveling with E. L. Beard built the first building at the crossroads: a tavern and inn, later Dave’s Saloon. It stood until the early 1980s
College
Washington College, described as the first industrial school in California, opened near the crossroads in 1871
Name
Named Irving in 1884, after a local circuit judge. It later became Irvington, reportedly after a railroad timetable misprint
Wine
The Palmdale Winery was among the largest in California until the 1906 earthquake. Its ruins stand near Five Corners at Washington Boulevard and Osgood Road
Fossils
Ice Age fossils dug from Irvington gravel pits gave their name to the Irvingtonian, a stage of North American mammal history
Today
Five Corners, where Fremont and Washington Boulevards meet, is marked at Irvington Plaza park

Source: Fremont, California (Wikipedia)

Mission San Jose

At the base of the eastern hills, below Mission Peak.

Ohlone
The mission was built at Oroysom, an Ohlone village. Thousands of Ohlone are buried in the mission cemetery, about a mile down Washington Boulevard
Founded
Mission San José was founded on June 11, 1797, the fourteenth Spanish mission in California. Its first adobe church was dedicated in 1809
Gold Rush
After the mission lands were sold off, H. C. Smith turned the mission into a store, saloon and hotel in 1848, and a post office opened in 1850
Earthquake
The Hayward Fault earthquake of October 21, 1868 shattered the walls of the adobe church
Restoration
The surviving adobe wing became a museum after restorations in 1915 and 1950. The church was rebuilt as a replica and rededicated on June 11, 1985
Today
Ohlone College is a block from the mission, and Mission Peak rises directly to the east

Sources: Mission San José (California) and Fremont, California (Wikipedia)

Warm Springs

The southern end of Fremont, where it meets Milpitas.

Rancho era
The town grew on Rancho Agua Caliente, granted to the Higuera family in 1839 and named for its warm springs
Name
Abram Harris settled here in 1858; the Harrisburgh post office opened in 1865 and was renamed Warm Springs in 1885. It was spelled “Warmsprings” from 1895 until 1950
Car plant
GM opened Fremont Assembly in South Fremont in 1962. Toyota and GM reopened it as NUMMI in 1984, the last car left on April 1, 2010, and Tesla reopened it as its Fremont factory in October 2010
BART
Warm Springs/South Fremont station opened on March 25, 2017
Roads
State Route 262 links I-880 and I-680 here

Sources: Fremont, California, Washington Township, NUMMI and Warm Springs/South Fremont station (Wikipedia)

Newark

A separate city that Fremont surrounds. I-880 runs along its eastern edge.

Incorporated
September 22, 1955, a few months before Fremont. A 1953 study had proposed one city of six towns, Newark included; Newark chose to incorporate on its own
Name
Chosen by J. Barr Robertson, after Newark Castle in Port Glasgow, Scotland
Railroad
The South Pacific Coast Railroad, finished after 1876 by Alfred Davis and James Fair, ran from Dumbarton Point south to Santa Cruz, and the town moved to the line
Carter Brothers
Thomas and Martin Carter moved their car works here in 1876 and built more than 700 cars for the South Pacific Coast. Their cars are preserved at Ardenwood Historic Farm in Fremont
Salt
Salt making on the bay shore began in the 1850s. Leslie Salt, now Cargill, still runs a refinery in Newark
Today
47,529 residents (2020 Census) on 13.9 square miles

Sources: Newark's Roots (City of Newark); Newark, California and Carter Brothers (Wikipedia)

Union City

Fremont's neighbor to the north, between the bay and the hills.

Formed
Alvarado and Decoto voted to merge on January 13, 1959. Tom Kitayama was the first mayor
Name
John and William Horner founded a settlement here in 1850 and named it after their Sacramento River steamboat, the Union
County seat
Alvarado was Alameda County's first county seat. The first courthouse opened there on June 6, 1853; the seat later moved to San Leandro
Sugar
E. H. Dyer's factory in Alvarado began processing beets on November 15, 1870: the nation's first successful beet sugar factory. California Historical Landmark No. 768 marks the site on Dyer Street
Decoto
Named for Ezra Decoto, who sold land to the railroad in 1867
Today
70,143 residents (2020 Census) on 19.2 square miles. BART has stopped here since the system opened in 1972

Sources: Union City, California, Alvarado, California and Decoto (Wikipedia); Landmark No. 768 (California Office of Historic Preservation)

Sunol

Over the hills, up Niles Canyon, where I-680 meets State Route 84. Sunol has never incorporated; it is part of unincorporated Alameda County.

Name
Antonio Suñol, born in Barcelona in 1797, who with three brothers-in-law received Rancho Valle de San José in 1839
Water Temple
Built in 1910 to a design by Willis Polk, after the Temple of Vesta in Tivoli. It marks where Alameda Creek, Arroyo de la Laguna and the Pleasanton wells met for the Spring Valley Water Company, San Francisco's water supplier before Hetch Hetchy. As of November 2026 the SFPUC lists it as closed during construction nearby
Rail
The restored 1884 Sunol depot is a stop on the Niles Canyon Railway
Mayor
A statue in front of the post office remembers Bosco, the dog the town elected mayor
Today
About 922 residents (2020 Census); Sunol Regional Wilderness is nearby

Sources: Sunol, California, Antonio Suñol and Sunol Water Temple (Wikipedia); Sunol Water Temple (SFPUC)

How it works now

Buying and selling changed in 2024 and 2025: buyers now sign a written agreement before touring, and every agent's pay is spelled out up front. Here's the whole path. Check things off as you go; your list stays in this browser.

  1. Talk it through

    A first conversation about what you want, where, and when. It costs nothing.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

  2. Get pre-approved

    A lender reviews your income, assets and credit and tells you how much you can borrow. A pre-approval makes an offer stronger. You pick the lender.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

  3. Sign a buyer agreement

    Before I show you a home, you and I sign a written buyer agreement. It states exactly how I'm paid. In California it can't run longer than three months without a renewal signed by both of us, and agent pay isn't set by law: it's negotiable.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

  4. Tour and make an offer

    I set up tours, pull recent comparable sales, and write your offer with the price and terms that protect you.

    Checklist 0 of 2 done

  5. Escrow and inspections

    Once your offer is accepted, escrow opens. You deposit earnest money, get inspections, and your lender orders an appraisal, all within the deadlines in your contract.

    Checklist 0 of 3 done

  6. Close

    You sign the loan and escrow documents, funds move through escrow, the deed records, and the keys are yours. Expect a supplemental property tax bill afterward. Never wire money on the strength of an email alone: call your escrow officer at a number you already have.
